#### 1. Microcap Fraud Task Force Core Priority: The "Shell & Asset Spin-Off" Scheme
The SEC's Microcap Fraud Task Force specifically targets schemes where corporate insiders use affiliated public shell entities to execute non-arm’s-length asset purchase agreements, creating artificial market valuations to distribute massive blocks of unregistered shares to retail investors.
* **The GTVH / SNTX Mechanism**: Javier Leal, acting as CEO of both GTVH and SNTX, orchestrated a transaction where GTVH transferred 100% of its operating subsidiaries (Go Fast Beverages, Snapt Beverages, Deep South Electric, Golden Era, and IP) into SNTX in exchange for **400,000,000 SNTX common shares** ($4,000,000 valuation).

#### 2. Section 5 Registration Violations: Abuse of SEC Rule 144
* **The Violation**: Item 3 of SNTX's June 15, 2026 Disclosure states:
  > *"The parties agree the holding period of the shares shall be under Rule 144 and applicable securities law. SNTX will transfer the shares to GTVH immediately. GTVH will be responsible for working with the Transfer Agent and FINRA to distribute to the individual shareholders as quickly as the regulations allow."*
* **Why Rule 144 is Illegally Invoked**:
  1. **Affiliate Status**: Javier Leal is a statutory affiliate and control person of both the issuer (SNTX) and the transferor (GTVH). Affiliates cannot use Rule 144 as a conduit for public spin-off distributions without complying with strict volume limits (Rule 144(e)), holding periods (Rule 144(d)), and current public information (Rule 144(c)).
  2. **Rule 144(i) Shell Prohibition**: Under Rule 144(i), securities issued by or to entities with shell characteristics cannot utilize Rule 144 safe harbors.
  3. **Section 5 Strict Liability**: Distributing 400M shares into the public market through transfer agents without an effective Form S-1 registration statement constitutes an ongoing, strict-liability violation of Section 5 of the Securities Act (*SEC v. Cavanagh*, 445 F.3d 105; *SEC v. CMKM Diamonds, Inc.*).

#### 3. Section 10(b) & Rule 10b-5: Scheme Liability & Material Misrepresentations
Under Supreme Court precedent (*Lorenzo v. SEC*, 139 S. Ct. 1094 (2019)), participating in a deceptive scheme to disseminate misleading corporate actions constitutes primary securities fraud under Rule 10b-5(a) and (c).
* **The Misleading "Fairness Statement"**: In Item 6 of the June 15 Disclosure, management stated that the Board determined the transaction was:
  > *"fair to SNTX and its shareholders while compensating the GTVH shareholders for the full value of their assets."*
* **The Material Omissions & Fraud**:
  1. **Concealment of Insolvency**: Management concealed that stripping 100% of operating assets rendered GTVH completely insolvent with $0 cash flow.
  2. **Concealment of 3-Month Debt Default**: Management failed to disclose that GTVH executed a binding $300,000 settlement with Bruce Perlowin on March 29, 2026, and had defaulted for three (3) consecutive months on 100% of payments (April, May, June, July 2026).
  3. **Concealment of Fraudulent Conveyance Encumbrance**: Management failed to disclose to SNTX public shareholders that the acquired operating assets are legally subject to immediate avoidance and clawback under Nevada Uniform Voidable Transactions Act (NRS 112.180).

#### 4. Section 20(a) Control Person Liability for Javier Leal
Javier Leal possessed direct managerial and operational control over both GTVH (CEO until Feb 2026) and SNTX (CEO/Director Nov 2025–Present). Under Section 20(a) of the Exchange Act, Leal is personally and jointly liable for all securities fraud committed by both corporations.
