#### 1. FINRA Rule 6490(d)(3) — Grounds for Deficiency Determination
Under FINRA Rule 6490(d)(3), FINRA Operations has express authority to issue a **Deficiency Determination** and refuse to process a corporate action if:
* **Rule 6490(d)(3)(1)**: The corporate action involves an issuer whose officers, directors, or controlling shareholders are the subject of pending litigation, investigation, or regulatory inquiries involving fraud or securities violations.
* **Rule 6490(d)(3)(2)**: FINRA has reason to believe the corporate action is related to an **unlawful distribution of securities** or a **scheme to defraud investors or creditors**.
* **Rule 6490(d)(3)(3)**: The issuer has failed to provide complete, accurate, or unmanipulated documentation regarding beneficial ownership and corporate debt status.

**Application**: SNTX and GTVH are attempting to use FINRA’s corporate action mechanism to consummate the distribution of 400,000,000 shares resulting directly from an illegal fraudulent conveyance under Nevada UVTA (NRS 112) and Texas TUFTA. FINRA must refuse processing.

#### 2. FINRA Rule 2010 — Failure to Observe Standards of Commercial Honor
FINRA Rule 2010 is the core standard governing the ethical conduct of market participants. Orchestrating a sham settlement to induce a creditor into an abeyance, immediately stripping 100% of corporate operating assets to a sister entity under identical insider control, defaulting for 3 consecutive months, and using OTC markets to wash the transaction through a 400M share issuance constitutes an egregious breach of commercial honor.

#### 3. FINRA Rule 2020 — Manipulative and Deceptive Contrivances
The APA and share distribution scheme constitutes a deceptive contrivance designed to:
1. Deceive creditors into believing GTVH would fulfill its $300,000 settlement obligations;
2. Deceive public market investors into buying SNTX and GTVH shares based on an artificial $4,000,000 asset transfer valuation ($0.01 per share); and

#### 4. FINRA Rule 3310 & BSA/AML Red Flags (Money Laundering & Layering)
FINRA Rule 3310 requires member firms and market infrastructure to identify and halt money laundering typologies:
* **Placement & Layering**: Moving productive corporate assets across related corporate shells (GTVH -> SNTX) to disguise ownership and defeat creditor attachment.
* **Integration**: Converting stripped physical assets into 400,000,000 publicly tradable OTC shares.
* **Federal Violation**: This scheme constitutes criminal money laundering under **18 U.S.C. §§ 1956(a)(1)(B)(i) and 1957** (monetary transactions > $10,000 derived from wire/securities fraud).

#### 5. Abuse of Rule 144 & FINRA Regulatory Notices 09-05 & 21-03
FINRA has repeatedly cautioned against microcap schemes where entities use asset transfers between affiliated shells to issue hundreds of millions of shares under claimed Rule 144 exemptions without registration. Because Javier Leal is an affiliate of both GTVH and SNTX, the 400M shares represent **control securities** subject to strict volume limits, holding periods, and mandatory registration.
